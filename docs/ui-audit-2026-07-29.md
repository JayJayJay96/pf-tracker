# PF Tracker — UI/UX Audit and Renovation Plan

Date: 2026-07-29
Scope: `app/`, `src/`, `docs/` on `main` @ 8794560. Excludes `.worktrees/` and `coverage/`.
Method: six independent specialist passes (dashboard visual, data-entry friction, IA/navigation, CSS architecture, accessibility, spec-vs-reality), 143 findings — 20 critical, 61 high, 51 medium, 11 low. Several criticals were reported independently by two or three passes that could not see each other's work; those are called out below as convergent.

---

## Verdict

The app is functionally complete and its domain layer is genuinely good — integer-sen money, tested allocation logic, RLS-scoped queries, real timezone handling. Nothing below is a criticism of the model underneath. The problem is entirely in the layer the user touches, and it has one root cause:

**There is no component layer.** Every screen is semantic HTML rendered directly, and all styling is delegated to one 520-line global stylesheet that targets bare element names scoped behind a negated ancestor selector: `.app-content > main:not(.dashboard-shell) form`, `... label`, `... li`, `... table`. Twenty-nine rules hang off that one `:not()`. This single decision produces most of what looks and feels wrong:

- The dashboard opted *into* a design (`.dashboard-shell`) and thereby opted every other screen *out* of it. Six of seven screens are styled by the generic bare-element fallback. They look unfinished because, structurally, they are.
- The sign-in screen — the first thing a user ever sees — sits outside `.app-content` entirely and so receives **zero** layout CSS. Its inputs get only base styling; its labels are unstyled inline text.
- With no components, there is nowhere for a field to own its own error, its own keyboard type, or its own formatting. So there is no field-level error anywhere in the app, and money entry falls back to a raw `<input>` policed by a regex.

The second root cause is narrower but does more daily damage: **the money format is validated at the wrong layer.** `parseRM` accepts exactly `/^RM(?:0|[1-9]\d*)\.\d{2}$/`, and that storage-layer strictness was pushed all the way out to the user's thumb as an HTML `pattern`. Everything about amount entry follows from that mistake.

## The worst problems

Ranked by daily pain, not by count.

**1. The amount field is physically untypeable on a phone.** *(convergent — 3 of 6 passes)*
`src/features/expenses/expense-view.tsx:36`, `src/features/bills/shared-bill-view.tsx:30`, `src/features/plan/monthly-plan-view.tsx:73` all ship `pattern="RM(?:0|[1-9][0-9]*)\.[0-9]{2}"` with `inputMode="decimal"`. `inputMode="decimal"` summons a numeric keypad. A numeric keypad has no letters. The field mandates a literal `RM` prefix. On the mobile PWA this app is built to be, the primary input of the primary flow cannot be completed without manually switching keyboards. To log RM12.50 you must type `R`, `M`, `1`, `2`, `.`, `5`, `0` — and `12.5`, `RM12.5`, `12.50`, `RM 12.50` and `rm12.50` are all rejected. This is the app's single worst defect.

**2. Any invalid input destroys the page and the user's typing.** *(convergent — 3 of 6 passes)*
Server actions signal every failure by `throw new Error('Invalid personal expense')` (`src/features/expenses/actions.ts:89`). An uncaught throw in a server action hits `app/(app)/error.tsx`, which replaces the entire screen with "We could not load your financial plan" — wrong copy for a rejected form, and it names no field. There is no `useActionState`, no `aria-invalid`, no field error anywhere in the app. Worse, the placeholder `RM0.00` *passes* the client `pattern` and then throws server-side on the `amountSen === 0` check, so the most likely thing a confused user types is also a guaranteed page wipe.

**3. Recurring income and commitments never carry forward.** *(functional, not cosmetic)*
`generate_monthly_plan` is an RPC fired only by the "Generate {month}" button on `/plan`. Nothing calls it automatically. So on the 1st of every month the dashboard hero reads **RM0.00** and the empty state says "Add templates in Monthly Plan, then generate it" — leaking internal vocabulary while asking the user to perform a manual bookkeeping chore before their own app will show them their own money. The spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-29-dashboard-first-ux-design.md:60`) explicitly promised "Income and commitments normally carry forward automatically into future months." It does not.

**4. Nothing on the dashboard is visual, and nothing is prioritized.** *(convergent — 2 of 6 passes)*
Grep the whole `src/` tree for `svg|progress|chart|meter|sparkline`: no hits. The dashboard is one 88px number followed by eleven identically-styled text cards in one flat `auto-fit` grid, no grouping, no heading, no landmark. Confirmed income sits at the same visual weight as "Pending requests". Meanwhile `src/domain/summary.ts:48-52` already computes a complete stacked breakdown of where the month's income went — the exact allocation bar that Monarch, YNAB and Copilot lead with — and the UI throws it away as flat text. The hero itself has no denominator, no days-remaining, no pacing, and — in a finance app — **no color change when it goes negative**. `formatSignedRM` exists solely to render that case and `.hero-amount` hard-codes one white. Overspending and a healthy surplus are visually identical apart from a hyphen.

**5. Four navigation clusters, seventeen links, six routes.** *(convergent — 3 of 6 passes)*
The header renders `.app-primary-nav` and `.app-secondary-nav`; the dashboard adds `.dashboard-nav`; and every other view renders its own `<nav aria-label="Primary">` with a *different link set on each page*. `/plan` is labeled "Income & Commitments", "Monthly Plan", and "Edit income and commitments" depending on where you click it. Three of six `.dashboard-nav` links use raw `<a href>` instead of `next/link`, forcing full document reloads. No page indicates which route is active, anywhere. The spec already called for exactly this cleanup at line 80 — "Page-level nav can be reduced or removed where it duplicates the header" — and it was never done.

**6. No focus indicator exists in the entire application.** *(convergent — 2 of 6 passes)*
`app/globals.css` contains no `:focus`, `:focus-visible`, or `outline` rule at all. Combined with input borders at roughly 1.6:1 contrast against their own background, a keyboard user cannot see where they are, and the fields are close to invisible even for a mouse user. There is also no skip link, so 9–15 repeated nav links precede the `<h1>` on every page. This is a straight WCAG 2.4.7 failure.

Immediately behind these: permanent deletion is gated only by a `<details><summary>` disclosure with a button styled identically to every other button; the 50/50-with-one-friend bill split — by far the most common real case — has no fast path and forces the full itemize-and-adjust machinery where every amount field is a bare untyped text input; and no expense categories are seeded (`supabase/seed.sql` is intentionally empty), so a brand-new user finds Save silently disabled with the fix in a section *below* the form.

## Renovation plan

Four phases. Each ships something usable alone. Ordered by pain relieved per unit of risk, not by architectural tidiness.

### Phase 1 — Make daily entry not hurt

No visual redesign in this phase. Pure input mechanics, on the existing markup.

**Money input.** Stop asking the user to format money. Build one `<MoneyInput>` client component and use it in all five places amounts are entered:
- `type="text"`, `inputMode="decimal"`, `autoComplete="off"`, `enterKeyHint="next"`, and no `pattern`.
- Render `RM` as static text *beside* the field, never inside its value.
- Accept anything a person would type: `5`, `5.5`, `12.50`, `1,250`, `RM12.50`, ` 12.5 `. Normalize on blur to `12.50`.
- Add `parseAmountInput(raw): { sen } | { error }` next to `src/domain/money.ts`. Keep `parseRM`/`formatRM` exactly as they are for storage and display — the strict pair is correct at that layer and the domain tests stay green. The new lenient parser is a UI-boundary adapter, nothing more.
- Optional and worth it: treat a bare integer as sen-free ringgit (`5` → RM5.00), which is what a keypad user means.

**Error feedback.** Convert the four action modules to return instead of throw. Wrap each form in `useActionState`. Each field renders its own message, gets `aria-invalid`, and — critically — **keeps its value**. Add one `aria-live="polite"` region per form for the summary. Do not let a validation failure reach `error.tsx` ever again; reserve that boundary for genuine load failures and fix its copy.

**Defaults and memory.** Read `profiles.default_payment_method` — it exists in the DB, is covered by SQL tests, and no `.ts`/`.tsx` file reads it; the form hardcodes `'tng'`. Default the date to today (already done on create, missing on edit). Remember the last category used and pre-select it. Seed a starter category set on first run so Save is never disabled on a fresh account — or, better, let the category field create-on-type so the concept of "set up categories first" disappears.

**The new add-expense flow, as the user experiences it:** open the app, tap the persistent Add button, land with the amount field already focused and the keypad already up. Type `12.50`. Type a description. Category and payment method are pre-filled from last time; date is today. Tap Save. Two typed fields, one tap. Notes and Merchant move behind a "More" disclosure so Save stays above the fold on a phone.

**Destructive actions.** Replace the `<details>` delete pattern with a real confirm step and give the confirming button its own danger styling. Same for `Archive` on plan templates, which is currently one unguarded click on a financial record.

Files: `src/domain/money.ts` (add, don't change), new `src/features/forms/money-input.tsx`, new `src/features/forms/field-error.tsx`, `src/features/{expenses,bills,plan,friends}/actions.ts`, all five `*-view.tsx`, `app/(app)/*/actions.ts`, `app/(app)/error.tsx`, `supabase/seed.sql`.

### Phase 2 — Styling foundation

**Choice: Tailwind v4.** Reasons specific to this codebase: the fatal property of the current sheet is that styling is addressed by *element name behind an ancestor negation*, so correctness depends on where a tag sits in the tree — Tailwind's utilities are addressed by the element itself, which structurally eliminates the entire bug class, including the unstyled sign-in page. Tailwind v4 configures in CSS with `@theme`, so the existing `:root` tokens port over nearly as-is. There is no `tailwind.config.js` churn and no PostCSS pipeline to hand-build in Next 16.

One caveat to raise explicitly: the dashboard-first spec lists "Do not add new dependencies" as a constraint. That constraint governed *that* change, not the project forever, and it is the reason the current sheet exists in this shape. This is your call. If you want to hold the line on zero dependencies, the fallback is CSS Modules — already native to Next, no dependency, and it fixes the scoping problem just as well; you lose the token ergonomics and write more CSS by hand. Either beats keeping the global sheet.

**Tokens to define:** a spacing scale (the sheet currently has 17 ad-hoc values), a radius scale (currently one, `8px`, everywhere), a type scale (currently marketing-sized clamps — a 68px `h1` on a data tool), semantic money colors — positive, negative, receivable, outflow, which a finance app cannot function without and this one entirely lacks — a visible focus ring, disabled styling (five buttons use `disabled` and none look it), and border colors that clear 3:1 so cards and inputs have edges.

Also: replace `font-family: Arial, Helvetica` with `next/font` — the framework ships it and it costs one import.

**Migration order, no big bang:** install alongside the existing sheet and let both run. Build the primitives first (`Button`, `Card`, `Field`, `Money`, `PageShell`). Convert sign-in first — it is currently the worst and the most isolated. Then one route per commit, deleting the corresponding `:not(.dashboard-shell)` rules as each route stops depending on them. The `:not()` block shrinks monotonically to zero and the dashboard's special-case class disappears with it.

### Phase 3 — Dashboard redesign

Top to bottom, mobile-first:

- **Compact header line.** Page name plus a month stepper — `‹ July 2026 ›` — replacing the current type-a-month-then-press-a-button form. Kill the 68px app-name `h1`; the app's name belongs in the header, and it is already there.
- **Hero card.** Remaining spendable, at a sane ~40–48px, with the context that makes it actionable: `RM1,842 of RM5,200 income · 9 days to payday · ~RM204/day`. Negative flips the whole card to the danger surface with honest copy ("Over by RM900 — commitments exceed confirmed income"). Add thousands separators; `formatRM` currently emits `RM5200.00`.
- **Allocation bar,** directly under the hero. One stacked horizontal bar of `confirmedIncome` split into commitments / savings / investments / spent / remaining. This is already computed in `summary.ts:48-52`. Plain CSS, no charting dependency. This is the single highest-value visual addition in the app.
- **Attention row,** conditional and only when non-zero: unresolved bills, pending requests, upcoming commitments — each a *link that drills through*, replacing today's dead counts. This is what replaces the hardcoded "Today's workflow" list, which currently occupies a full card and ignores today entirely.
- **Four primary tiles:** Income, Committed, Spent, Friends owe — semantic color, larger, with a month-over-month delta.
- **Grouped secondary metrics** behind a heading, smaller: allocations and friend details. Cut "plan snapshots loaded" (internal telemetry), fold "Total cash outflow" and "Paid on behalf" into the friends group with real definitions, and move "Days to next salary" into the hero line where it belongs instead of a numeric card holding the sentence "Not available".
- **Category breakdown,** bottom, bar-per-category.

Eleven flat cards become one bar, four tiles, a conditional attention row, and one grouped section.

Mobile: single column, hero above the fold — today it is pushed below by stacked navigation.

### Phase 4 — Navigation and shell

- **One nav model.** Delete `.dashboard-nav` and every per-view `<nav aria-label="Primary">`. The header is the only navigation.
- **Mobile: bottom tab bar** — Dashboard, Expenses, Bills, More — plus a persistent **Add** FAB, because "log an expense in five seconds at a checkout counter" is this app's whole reason to exist and it currently takes 8+ interactions from cold open.
- **Active state** via `usePathname()`, everywhere.
- **Convert the raw `<a href>` links** in `summary-view.tsx:83,85,86` to `next/link`.
- **One canonical label per route.** `/plan` is "Income & Commitments" everywhere.
- Add a skip link, per-route `metadata` titles (every route currently shares one document title), and fix `app/manifest.ts`, whose cream/green colors contradict the dark navy UI and flash on launch.
- Style `loading.tsx` and `error.tsx`, both currently bare unstyled sentences with copy that is wrong for most routes.

## Constraints the renovation must not break

- **Money stays integer sen.** `parseRM`/`formatRM` keep their strict contract; lenient parsing is a new UI-boundary adapter, not a change to the domain pair.
- **`transaction_date` vs `recorded_at` stay distinct.** Reports key off transaction date. Do not collapse them in any redesign of a list row.
- **Asia/Kuala_Lumpur** for all "today" derivation; keep the `Intl` formatting approach in `app/(app)/page.tsx:16-34`.
- **Drafts are localStorage-only, versioned, per-user, cleared on save** (README privacy section). Do not widen what is persisted client-side.
- **No service worker caching of authenticated pages, API responses, or exports.**
- **RLS is owner-scoped**; all reads stay server-authorized. No new client-side data fetching of financial rows.
- **Do not change finance calculations or the DB schema** as part of the visual work. The carry-forward fix in Phase 1 is a scheduling/trigger change, not a recalculation.

## Test blast radius

208 selector-based assertions across 16 files.

- **Component tests (~132 assertions, 9 files).** `summary-view.test.tsx` is the big one at 42 and will need substantial rewriting, since the metric list it asserts on is exactly what Phase 3 restructures. `friends-view` (21), `monthly-plan-view` (17), `report-view` (14), `expense-view` (12), `protected-layout` (12) need moderate updates — mostly nav and label changes.
- **E2E (7 specs).** `shared-bill.spec.ts` is the heaviest by far (89 matching lines) and is tightly coupled to the current bill-resolution markup, so the Phase 1 fast-path work will hit it hard. `monthly-plan-dashboard.spec.ts` (46) and `payment-request.spec.ts` (42) follow.
- Phase 1 breaks tests that assert the `RM0.00` placeholder and the literal-`RM` amount format. Phase 4 breaks anything asserting on duplicated nav links — which is most of `protected-layout.test.tsx`.
- Worth doing first: the assertions lean on exact visible copy. Migrating the noisiest ones to `getByRole`/`getByLabelText` before Phase 3 will stop the redesign from cascading through the suite twice.

## What I recommend doing first

Phase 1's money input, on its own, in one commit. It is the defect you feel every single time you use the app, it is currently a hard blocker on mobile rather than an annoyance, it is contained to one new component plus one new parser function, and it does not require settling the Tailwind question or touching the domain layer. Fix the error handling in the same pass — returning instead of throwing is what makes a wrong amount survivable — and the app goes from "painful to key in" to "usable" before any pixel changes. The visual renovation is real work and worth doing, but it is the second problem, not the first.

### Dashboard visual design & information hierarchy

_24 findings._ The dashboard is not a designed screen — it is a stack of semantic HTML bands with a dark palette applied on top. The strongest evidence: one shared card recipe covers alerts, the hero, an instruction list and metrics alike (app/globals.css:366-374); the font stack is literally Arial/Helvetica (globals.css:31) in a Next.js app with next/font unused; there is no desktop layout at all, only the mobile column stretched to 1180px; and spacing is a pile of unrelated magic numbers with no scale. Information hierarchy fails at the top and the bottom: a 68px h1 renders the app's own name while eleven equal-weight metric cards render the actual data in identical white, ungrouped, with no heading, and with a final row that always contains a stretched orphan card. There is zero data visualization anywhere in src/ — no progress bar, no allocation bar, no trend — despite src/domain/summary.ts:48-52 already computing a complete stacked breakdown of where the month's income went, which is exactly what a Monarch or Copilot home screen leads with. The hero number is contextless (no denominator, no days remaining, no pacing), has no visual state for going negative even though formatSignedRM exists to produce one, is explained by a sentence that names one of its four deductions, and is rendered at 88px through a formatter that emits no thousands separators (src/domain/money.ts:25-30). Copy is filler or leaked internals throughout — 'Monthly control room', a hardcoded 'Today's workflow' list that ignores today while live attention-worthy counts sit unused in flat cards, and '{n} plan snapshots loaded' shipping a raw row count in vocabulary the project's own spec says to hide. Four nav clusters and 17 links cover six routes, with /plan named three different things; the spec at docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-29-dashboard-first-ux-design.md:80 already called for removing the duplicate page-level nav and it was never done, so several of these are unfinished implementation rather than debatable taste.

#### [CRITICAL] No data visualization anywhere on the dashboard
`src/features/dashboard/summary-view.tsx:133`

**Problem.** The entire dashboard is text. Grepping the whole src/ tree for `svg|progress|chart|bar|meter|sparkline` returns one unrelated hit in src/lib/supabase/monthly-plan.ts — there is not a single progress bar, allocation bar, donut, sparkline, or budget-vs-actual visual in the product. The dashboard is a hero number (summary-view.tsx:109) plus 11 text cards (summary-view.tsx:133-140). The data for at least three charts is already computed and thrown away as flat text: `confirmedIncome / activeCommitments / savings / investments / resolvedPersonalSpending` (src/domain/summary.ts:48-52) is a complete stacked allocation bar of where this month's income went; `daysToNextSalary` (queries.ts:401) plus `remainingSpendable` is a pacing gauge; `upcomingCommitmentsSen` is a forward-looking reserved slice.

**Impact.** The user cannot see at a glance whether they are on track — only whether a number is big or small with no baseline. Every comparison (is RM800 remaining good? how much of my income is already committed? am I spending faster than the month is elapsing?) requires mental arithmetic across cards. This is the single largest gap versus Monarch/YNAB/Copilot, where the home screen is a bar or ring before it is a number.

**Fix.** Add three visuals with plain CSS (no dependency needed, per the spec's no-new-deps constraint): (1) a stacked horizontal allocation bar directly under the hero — commitments / savings / investments / spent / remaining as segments of confirmedIncome, with a legend showing each amount; (2) a pacing bar comparing % of income spent against % of the month elapsed (or days-to-salary), which is what makes 'remaining spendable' actionable; (3) a per-category spend breakdown (categories already exist on the expenses feature). Segments should use semantic hues, not all --blue.

#### [CRITICAL] Eleven flat, equal-weight metric cards with no grouping and no headline
`src/features/dashboard/summary-view.tsx:43`

**Problem.** `metrics` is a flat 11-item tuple array (lines 43-58) rendered into an undifferentiated `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(210px, 1fr))` grid (globals.css:455-460). Every card gets identical treatment: same border, same background, same `dt` at 0.84rem muted, same `dd` at 1.35rem weight 750 in the same white (globals.css:462-480). Income sits visually equal to 'Pending requests'. The grid has no heading, no `aria-label`, no period caption — 11 numbers just appear at the bottom of the page with nothing saying what they are or what month they cover. There is no grouping even though the metrics fall into obvious clusters: money in (confirmed income), planned out (commitments, savings, investments), actually spent (personal spending, total cash outflow), friends (friends owe, paid on behalf, pending requests), and forward-looking (upcoming commitments, days to salary).

**Impact.** The user scans a wall of near-identical white numbers and cannot tell which two or three matter. Nothing is emphasized, so nothing is read. This is the concrete cause of the user's 'main UI needs serious renovation' complaint.

**Fix.** Cut to 4-5 primary tiles (Income, Committed, Spent, Remaining, Friends owe) shown at a larger size with semantic color and a delta-vs-last-month line, then move the rest into two labelled grouped sections ('Allocations' and 'With friends') with a heading each and a smaller card size. Give the grid a real `<h2>`. Use color: income/receivable in a positive hue, outflow in a warm hue.

#### [CRITICAL] The hero number has no denominator, no context, and no visual state for going negative
`src/features/dashboard/summary-view.tsx:104`

**Problem.** The hero (lines 104-113) renders `formatSignedRM(summary.remainingSpendable)` and nothing else: no 'of RM X income', no days remaining, no per-day safe-to-spend, no progress indicator, no comparison to last month. Worse, `.hero-amount` has a single hard-coded `color: var(--text)` (globals.css:401-407), so a negative remaining spendable — the most urgent signal the app can produce, and the reason `formatSignedRM` exists at summary-view.tsx:25-27 — renders in the exact same calm white as a healthy surplus. The only change is a leading hyphen at 88px. The surrounding copy ('Available this month', 'conservative guide') stays reassuring even when the value is -RM900.

**Impact.** A user who has overspent sees no alarm — no red, no icon, no changed message, no changed subtitle. And a user in the black gets a bare number they cannot act on because there is no 'for how many days' or 'out of how much'.

**Fix.** Branch the hero on sign: negative gets a warning surface, warm/red numerals, and copy like 'Over by RM900 — commitments exceed confirmed income'. On the positive path add a second line 'RM X of RM Y income · Z days to payday · ~RM N/day', driven by the already-computed `confirmedIncome` and `daysToNextSalary`, plus the allocation bar.

#### [HIGH] h1 clamp scales to 4.25rem (68px) to display the app's own name
`app/globals.css:316`

**Problem.** `.dashboard-shell h1 { font-size: clamp(2rem, 5vw, 4.25rem); line-height: 1 }` (globals.css:316-320) is applied to the string 'Personal Finance Tracker' (summary-view.tsx:65) — the app name, which is already in the header as 'PF Tracker' (app/(app)/layout.tsx:29-31). At any viewport ≥1360px the 5vw term is capped, so desktop users get a 68px, three-word, zero-information headline occupying roughly 70px of prime above-the-fold space, styled larger relative to its importance than anything else on the page except the hero. Note the clamp is keyed to `vw` while the shell is capped at `max-width: 1180px` (globals.css:287-291), so the type keeps growing on ultrawide screens after the layout has stopped — type size and column width decouple. On a 375px phone the 2rem floor wins and 'Personal Finance Tracker' at 32px/1.0 wraps to 2-3 lines, another ~96px of dead vertical space above the number the user came for.

**Impact.** The largest text on the screen after the hero tells the user nothing they didn't know. It pushes the actual content down, on mobile far enough to matter.

**Fix.** The page title should be the month, not the app name: make `<h1>` the month label at ~1.75-2rem, drop the 'Personal Finance Tracker' string entirely (the header brand covers it), and delete the 5vw/4.25rem clamp. If a fluid clamp is kept, key it to a container query or a fixed rem ramp, not `vw` on a max-width-capped column.

#### [HIGH] hero-amount clamp reaches 5.5rem (88px) while amounts have no thousands separators
`app/globals.css:401`

**Problem.** `.hero-amount { font-size: clamp(2.4rem, 8vw, 5.5rem); font-weight: 800; line-height: 0.95 }` (globals.css:401-407) is combined with `formatRM` in src/domain/money.ts:25-30, which emits `RM${ringgit}.${sen}` with no grouping at all. So a realistic monthly figure renders as 'RM12345.67' at 88px in Arial Black-weight — an unbroken 10-glyph digit run the eye cannot chunk. There is also no `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` anywhere in globals.css, so digits are proportional; combined with `line-height: 0.95` on 88px text the number sits cramped against the h2 above it.

**Impact.** The most important number in the app is genuinely hard to read at a glance — the user has to count digits to know if it is RM1,234 or RM12,345. Enormous type does not compensate for missing separators; it amplifies the problem.

**Fix.** Add a grouped formatter (Intl.NumberFormat('en-MY', { style:'currency', currency:'MYR' }) or manual grouping in money.ts, keeping the strict parse path untouched) and use it for display. Cap the hero at ~3-3.5rem, add `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` to money elements globally, raise line-height to ~1.05, and consider de-emphasizing the sen with a smaller span.

#### [HIGH] Four separate nav/link clusters on one screen, 17 links, six routes duplicated 2-3x under different names
`src/features/dashboard/summary-view.tsx:82`

**Problem.** The dashboard shows four link clusters: `.app-primary-nav` (4 links, layout.tsx:32-37), `.app-secondary-nav` (3 links, layout.tsx:38-42), `.dashboard-nav` (6 links, summary-view.tsx:82-89), `.quick-actions` (4 links, summary-view.tsx:115-122). 17 links to 6 destinations. `/plan` appears three times under three different names — 'Income & Commitments' (layout.tsx:39), 'Monthly Plan' (summary-view.tsx:83), 'Edit income and commitments' (summary-view.tsx:120). `/expenses` likewise: 'Expenses', 'Personal Expenses', 'Add personal expense'. `/transactions` three times, `/shared-bills` three times. The project's own spec explicitly required this cleanup — docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-29-dashboard-first-ux-design.md:80 says 'Page-level nav can be reduced or removed where it duplicates the header' — and `.dashboard-nav` was never removed, so this is an unfinished implementation, not a judgement call.

**Impact.** The user cannot build a mental model of the app because the same page has three names. Screen real estate above the hero is consumed by redundant chrome, and the quick-action row loses its meaning as 'the two things you do daily' because six other buttons look just as prominent.

**Fix.** Delete `.dashboard-nav` (summary-view.tsx:82-89) outright — the header already covers all six routes. Cut `.quick-actions` to the two recording actions the spec names as primary ('Add expense', 'Add shared bill') and move the income/commitments CTA into the hero where the spec put it (spec lines 43-44). Standardize one label per route across header, dashboard, and buttons.

#### [HIGH] Three of six dashboard-nav links use raw <a> instead of next/link
`src/features/dashboard/summary-view.tsx:83`

### Styling architecture & visual system

_17 findings._ This is not a stylesheet that needs polishing — it is a prototype-grade sheet that never acquired a design system, and its central architectural choice is actively unsafe. 520 lines carry the entire product, and 29 of its rule blocks (lines 139–285) hang off `.app-content > main:not(.dashboard-shell)`: bare-element styling gated on a negated class on a grandparent. That selector already fails in production — `app/auth/sign-in/page.tsx:14` and `app/(app)/loading.tsx` sit outside `.app-content`, so the first screen every user sees is genuinely unstyled HTML flush against the viewport corner. It survives elsewhere only because React emits no wrapper nodes; one `<div>` added for a Suspense or error boundary silently deletes the layout of seven screens with no error and no failing test.

The token layer is 10 colour variables and nothing else: no spacing scale (17 ad-hoc rem values including indistinguishable 0.7/0.75 and 0.8/0.85 pairs), no radius scale (`border-radius: 8px` written out 11 times), no type scale, no elevation (`box-shadow` appears 0 times in the file), no motion, no focus ring. Two of the ten tokens (`--panel`, `--warning`) are referenced zero times while their values are hand-re-encoded as raw rgba elsewhere. Four findings are outright WCAG failures: no `:focus`/`:focus-visible`/`outline` rule exists anywhere, so keyboard entry of a 6-field expense form is unguided (SC 2.4.7/2.4.13); card and row borders composite to ~1.38–1.55:1 against their interiors, well under the 3:1 of SC 1.4.11, which is why lists read as an undifferentiated wall; `color-scheme: dark` is forced with no light mode in a PWA meant for keying in expenses outdoors; and `display: block` on `<table>` risks stripping table semantics while clipping money columns with no scroll affordance. Most damning for the domain: there is no green, red, positive, negative, success or danger colour, and no semantic class in any of the 22 `className` values in the codebase — so `remainingSpendable` at −RM800 renders in the same white, at the same 88px, as +RM800 (`summary-view.tsx:109` → `globals.css:402`), distinguished by one hyphen. That is the entire glanceable premise of Monarch/Copilot/YNAB, absent.

Recommended target architecture: **stay with a single global CSS sheet, but harden it into a real token + component-class system — do not adopt Tailwind here.** The reasoning is specific to this repo. Scale argues against a migration: 8 routes, ~10 view components, 520 lines of CSS, 22 total class names. #### [CRITICAL] 29 rules gated on a negated-ancestor selector make every new page silently unstyled
`app/globals.css:139`

**Problem.** Lines 139–285 contain 29 separate rule blocks all prefixed with `.app-content > main:not(.dashboard-shell)` (verified count: 29 occurrences, one per selector block). This is the entire layout system for 7 of the 8 screens, and it depends on four simultaneous structural coincidences: (1) an ancestor with class `app-content`, (2) `main` being its DIRECT child (`>`), (3) `main` NOT carrying `.dashboard-shell`, and (4) React components emitting no wrapper DOM node. Every one of these is invisible to the person writing a new page. Concretely: `app/(app)/layout.tsx:47` renders `<div className="app-content">{children}</div>`, and each view returns a bare `<main>` (e.g. `src/features/expenses/expense-view.tsx:103`, `src/features/friends/friends-view.tsx:26`). The `>` combinator only works because Next.js route-group layouts happen not to insert a node between them. Wrap a view in a `<div>` for any reason — a client-side error boundary, a Suspense wrapper, a transition container — and all 29 rules die at once: forms lose `display:flex`, labels lose `display:grid`, sections lose their card border, lists lose `list-style:none`, inputs lose `width:100%`. Nothing errors; the page just renders as raw 1995 HTML. The same happens the moment anyone adds a second styled shell class, because the negation `:not(.dashboard-shell)` enumerates exclusions by hand — a new `.reports-shell` would have to be added to all 29 selectors.

**Impact.** The styling of every screen is one refactor away from total collapse, with no test or type error to catch it. This is already happening in production (see the sign-in and loading findings). For the user it means the app looks arbitrarily broken on some screens and not others, with no discoverable reason.

**Fix.** Invert the model: delete all 29 negated selectors and give layout to explicit, additive classes. Introduce a `.page` / `.page-section` / `.field` / `.data-list` / `.data-table` set of component classes applied at the component that owns the markup, so styling travels with the JSX instead of with DOM position. Keep exactly one global scope for true resets (box-sizing, font, body). Concretely: add `className="page"` to each `<main>` (and `page page--dashboard` for the dashboard), replace `.app-content > main:not(.dashboard-shell) form` with `.field-row`, `... label` with `.field`, `... li` with `.data-list li`, and so on. #### [MEDIUM] display:block on <table> breaks column layout and table semantics, with no scroll affordance
`app/globals.css:255`

**Problem.** Lines 255–261 apply `display: block; overflow-x: auto; width: 100%` directly to the `table` element, plus `white-space: nowrap` on every `th`/`td` (263–269). ### Data-entry friction

_32 findings._ Data-entry friction is the worst dimension of this app, and the root cause is a single decision: the domain's strict display format `^RM(?:0|[1-9]\d*)\.\d{2}$` (src/domain/money.ts:4) was pushed all the way out to the input element as an HTML `pattern`, so users must key in the currency symbol by hand. Paired with `inputMode=\"decimal\"`, which summons a keypad with no letter keys, the amount field on the four forms that matter is not merely tedious but literally unfillable by typing on an iPhone — and this is a PWA. Everything downstream compounds it: no `title` so the validation bubble says only \"Please match the requested format\", a placeholder (`RM0.00`) that passes client validation and then throws server-side, and zero field-level error reporting anywhere in the codebase (no `useActionState`, no `aria-invalid`) so every rejection replaces the whole screen with \"We could not load your financial plan\" — a message about loading, when the user was writing. Beyond the amount field, the app remembers nothing (category resets every save, `profiles.default_payment_method` is in the schema but never read, no datalist of the user's own merchants, no repeat-last-expense), no starter categories are seeded so the first expense is blocked behind a silently disabled button whose explanation is off-screen, and the commonest shared-bill case — 50/50 with one friend — has a working domain function that no UI calls, forcing seven interactions and re-typing a description the app already stored. Finally, the single global button style and identical section/form styling in globals.css mean the add form, the category form, the ten-field filter form, and \"Confirm permanent deletion\" all look the same, and destructive deletion is gated by nothing more than a `<details><summary>` toggle. Fixing the amount input, adding inline per-field errors, and seeding defaults would remove most of the user's stated pain; the rest is remembering what they did last time.

#### [CRITICAL] Amount field is physically untypeable on a phone: numeric keypad cannot produce the mandatory "RM" prefix
`src/features/expenses/expense-view.tsx:36`

**Problem.** The amount input is `<input name="amount" inputMode="decimal" pattern="RM(?:0|[1-9][0-9]*)\.[0-9]{2}" placeholder="RM0.00" required>` (expense-view.tsx:36-43; identical at shared-bill-view.tsx:27-33, monthly-plan-view.tsx:69-78 and monthly-plan-view.tsx:294-303). The HTML `pattern` attribute is implicitly anchored, so the value must literally begin with the two letters `RM`. **Fix.** Drop the currency prefix from the input value entirely. ### Design intent vs shipped reality

_20 findings._ The specs and the shipped app diverge in a consistent direction: the two design docs were treated as markup-and-ordering checklists, satisfied literally by the assertions in each plan's test step, and stopped there. Task 1 of the 2026-07-29 plan added a header nav but never did the "reduce page-level nav" half of the spec, so the app now has *more* navigation than before the UX cleanup. Task 5 renamed the plan page but not the dashboard copy that points at it, so "Monthly Plan / templates / snapshots" still greets a first-time user. The 2026-07-28 dark-UI work explicitly scoped itself to "the protected app shell and dashboard only" and that scope was never followed up, which is the mechanical reason six of seven screens are unstyled semantic HTML. Underneath the cosmetics there are two genuine product-level gaps that no amount of restyling will fix: recurring income and commitments do not carry forward without a manual "Generate <Month>" press (spec line 60 and vision §18 both say they should), and every money field demands the domain's exact `RM12.34` string on a numeric keypad while validation failures throw into the global error boundary. Those three — carry-forward, money input, inline errors — are the whole of "not user friendly to key in" and should be fixed before any visual work.

=== HARD CONSTRAINTS A RENOVATION MUST NOT BREAK ===

1. Integer-sen money storage. `src/domain/money.ts` defines `Sen = number` and every amount is a non-negative safe integer of sen. `assertNonnegativeSen` (money.ts:6) throws on non-integers AND on negatives, and `formatRM` calls it (money.ts:26) — so **`formatRM` throws on any negative value**. Two views already work around this and must keep doing so: `formatSignedRM` in summary-view.tsx:25-27 (renders `-RM1.00`, ASCII hyphen) and `formatSignedAmount` in report-view.tsx:35-38 (renders `−RM12.50`, U+2212 minus). Note those two use *different* minus characters and the tests assert both — do not "unify" them without updating summary-view.test.tsx:80 and report-view.test.tsx:103. Never introduce float arithmetic on amounts; use `addSen`/`subtractSen` (money.ts:33-46), and note `subtractSen` is the only function permitted to return a negative.

2. RM formatting is a strict canonical form, not a locale format. `formatRM` emits `RM<integer>.<2 digits>` with **no thousands separator** — RM5000.00, not RM5,000.00. `parseRM` (money.ts:13) accepts only `/^RM(?:0|[1-9]\d*)\.\d{2}$/` — no leading zeros, no spaces, no separators. Unit and e2e tests assert the exact strings (`RM3000.00`, `RM5250.50`, `RM80.40`, `value="RM80.40"`). You may add a *lenient input coercion* layer in front of `parseRM` (recommended, see findings) but you may not loosen `parseRM` itself or change `formatRM`'s output shape without touching a large number of assertions and the CSV/JSON export routes.

3. Transaction-date vs recorded-at semantics. README line 5-6 and vision §17.4 are explicit: the **transaction date determines the reporting period**; `recorded_at` is audit-only. `sumInPeriod` in `src/domain/summary.ts:27-31` filters exclusively on `transactionDate` via `isDateInPeriod`. Backdating must keep working: the expense form's `transactionDate` is user-editable and defaults to today (expense-view.tsx:53-61), and both Expenses and Reports display recorded-at separately (expense-view.tsx:195-198; report-view.tsx:223-228). Any redesign that collapses these two dates into one, or that starts filtering on `recorded_at`, silently rewrites history. Reports also state this rule in visible copy (report-view.tsx:64-67).

4. Asia/Kuala_Lumpur is the authoritative "today". Every page computes the current date/month server-side with `Intl.DateTimeFormat('en-CA', { timeZone: 'Asia/Kuala_Lumpur' })` — app/(app)/page.tsx:16-34 (`currentMonthStart`, `currentDate`), app/(app)/plan/page.tsx:24-33, app/(app)/shared-bills/page.tsx:15-21 (`todayInMalaysia`), and the same helper is duplicated in the expenses/reports pages. `en-CA` is chosen because it yields `YYYY-MM-DD`. Never replace these with `new Date().toISOString()` or any client-side date, and never move "today" resolution into a client component — a user near midnight would get the wrong period. Two *different* timezone conventions coexist deliberately and must be preserved: **date-only values are formatted with `timeZone: 'UTC'`** (summary-view.tsx:30-34, monthly-plan-view.tsx:23-27, report-view.tsx:9-15 all parse `${date}T00:00:00Z`) so a stored ISO date never shifts a day, whereas **`recorded_at` timestamps are formatted in Asia/Kuala_Lumpur** (expense-view.tsx:86-92). Mixing these up shifts dates by one.

5. Period is the calendar month, first-to-last day, and `periodStart` must literally be the 1st. `getCalendarMonth` (periods.ts:28) is the only period source; `getDashboardSummary` throws `'Period start must be the first day of a calendar month'` (dashboard/queries.ts:231-233) and `getMonthlyPlan` does the same (plan/queries.ts:168-172). `isDateInPeriod` (periods.ts:38-47) re-derives the month from `startDate` and throws `'Invalid calendar period'` if the boundaries aren't a clean calendar month — so you cannot pass a salary-cycle or rolling-30-day window through the existing domain layer. Vision §8 resolved this as MVP scope ("A configurable salary-cycle mode may be considered in a future version"); a renovation must not smuggle one in.

6. Snapshot/plan generation model. Templates are recurring definitions; `plan_entries` are dated, immutable-per-month snapshots. Vision §17.5 and the design spec state a template edit **only affects later generated months** — the e2e spec proves this behaviour end to end (monthly-plan-dashboard.spec.ts:77-90: editing Salary to RM5500 leaves July at RM3300 and makes August RM3500). Generation is idempotent by migration (20260728030000_secure_idempotent_plan_entries). Each entry carries both `amount_sen` (planned) and `actual_amount_sen` (actual), and every consumer prefers actual when present — `entry.actualAmountSen ?? entry.amountSen` at dashboard/queries.ts:292 and again at :397. Preserve the planned/actual distinction in any redesigned entry row; do not collapse them into one field.

7. Conservative-spendable calculation is settled and must not change. `remainingSpendable = confirmedIncome − (activeCommitments + savings + investments + resolvedPersonalSpending)` (summary.ts:53-57). Commitments subtract whether or not they are paid (vision §33.6). Pending income is excluded (§6.1). Savings and investments are treated as commitments (§6.3). Unresolved shared bills count toward cash outflow but **not** toward personal spending (§33.3) — implemented at dashboard/queries.ts:337-347 (only `kind === 'user'` portions of `resolved` bills enter `personalSpending`) while `sharedCashOutflow` sums all bills (:355-358). Friend repayments are collections, never income (§23.6). Both implementation plans list "Do not change finance calculations" as a global constraint; the UI may re-present these numbers but must not re-derive them.

8. Draft/localStorage privacy rule (README lines 40-46). Only *unsaved form fields* may be stored locally, under versioned keys isolated by authenticated user and form, and a successful save clears the draft. `src/lib/drafts.ts` implements the key as `pf-tracker:draft:v1:<encoded userId>:<encoded formId>` and validates `version` + all-string values on load, wiping the entry on any mismatch. `DraftForm` (src/features/forms/draft-form.tsx) persists on `onInput` and calls `clearDraft` + `form.reset()` after the action resolves. Renovation rules: never persist fetched/server data (balances, transactions, friend names, request totals) to localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB or any cache; never widen the key to be user-agnostic; bump `DRAFT_VERSION` if the field set of a form changes so stale drafts self-purge. If validation becomes non-throwing (recommended above), `clearDraft` must move behind a success check — clearing on a failed submit would destroy the user's input.

9. No service worker, ever, in this phase. README: "Authenticated pages, API responses, and exports stay server-authorized and are not cached by a service worker. The app currently installs from its web manifest without registering a service worker." A grep for `serviceWorker` across app/ and src/ returns nothing, and this is deliberate — a SW cache would persist owner-scoped financial data to disk. `app/manifest.ts` is manifest-only and must stay that way. This directly contradicts vision §28 (app-shell caching, offline entry, sync status), and the README/design-spec position is the current authority (design spec: offline sync is "a later, separately tested phase"; vision §33.7 stages it). A renovation may improve perceived performance with server-side streaming and `loading.tsx`, not with client caching. Also note every protected page sets `export const dynamic = 'force-dynamic'` (app/(app)/layout.tsx:8) — do not add `revalidate`, ISR, or `unstable_cache` to authenticated routes.

10. Owner-scoped RLS and the server boundary. Every user-owned row carries `user_id` and RLS restricts access to the owner (design spec Architecture; enabled in migrations 20260728000000, ...020000, ...040000, ...050000, ...080000). All writes go through `'use server'` actions that call `requireCurrentUserId(() => client.auth.getClaims())` before touching a repository (app/(app)/plan/actions.ts:44-48 pattern, repeated per feature), and the userId is passed explicitly into every repository call. Do not introduce client-side Supabase reads/writes for UI convenience, do not accept a `userId` from a form field, and keep `getProtectedRouteRedirect` in the (app) layout as the single auth gate. Export routes under app/api/export/* are generated on demand for the signed-in owner (report-view.tsx:236-244) and must not become cacheable or shareable URLs.

11. Locked/immutable states are load-bearing. Resolved shared-bill allocations are locked (migrations ...060000 harden, ...070000 close insert bypasses; surfaced as "Resolved shared allocations are locked" in transaction-history-view.tsx:168 and "View locked shared bill" at :33-36). Portions included in a pending payment request are locked until the request is cancelled (vision §15.3); FriendLedgerView only offers `status === 'unrequested'` portions for a new request (friends-view.tsx:63, :91). Requests are paid in full only, with the amount pinned as a hidden `formatRM(request.totalSen)` field (friends-view.tsx:200-204). Rounding residue goes to the user by default (vision §33.2). Participant portions must sum exactly to the bill total (§12.3) — `allocateBill` enforces this and ResolutionEditor surfaces the failure as `role="alert"` (resolution-editor.tsx:347). Any new UI affordance that appears to let the user edit a locked bill or partially settle a request will be rejected server-side; do not build the affordance.

12. Also worth respecting even though it is only a plan-level constraint: **no new dependencies** (both implementation plans list it; package.json dependencies are literally next/react/react-dom/@supabase/*/zod). No CSS framework, no charting library, no icon package, no component library. A visual renovation has to be hand-rolled CSS in app/globals.css plus inline SVG. There is currently exactly one stylesheet and no CSS modules.

#### [CRITICAL] Recurring income/commitments never carry forward — dashboard reads RM0.00 until the user manually presses "Generate <Month>"
`src/features/plan/monthly-plan-view.tsx:242`

**Problem.** The 2026-07-29 spec (line 60) states "Income and commitments normally carry forward automatically into future months", and the vision doc §18 says rollover generates recurring salary/commitments/savings/investments at the start of each period. Shipped behaviour is the opposite: `getMonthlyPlan` only returns entries that already exist for `periodStart`, and the only way to create them is the `<form action={actions?.generate}><button>Generate {label}</button></form>` at monthly-plan-view.tsx:242-245 wired to `generateMonthAction` (app/(app)/plan/actions.ts). `getDashboardSummary` derives `hasSnapshots` purely from `entryResult.data.length > 0` (src/features/dashboard/queries.ts:390), so on the 1st of every new month the hero drops to RM0.00 and the dashboard shows "No plan snapshots for this month" (summary-view.tsx:92-94) even though the user's templates are all still active.

**Impact.** Every month the app silently forgets the user's whole financial setup. The single number the product exists to show — remaining spendable — reads RM0.00 until the user remembers to navigate to a secondary page and press a button whose name ("Generate July 2026") gives no hint that it is mandatory. This is the most likely reason the app feels broken rather than merely ugly.

**Fix.** Auto-generate the current period's entries on first read of the dashboard/plan for a month that has active templates (the existing `generateMonthlyPlan` is already idempotent per the 20260728030000_secure_idempotent_plan_entries migration, so calling it lazily is safe). Keep a visible "regenerate" escape hatch on the plan page for corrections, but the empty state must never be the default for a user who has templates. If lazy generation on a GET is unacceptable, at minimum turn the empty state into an inline one-click "Set up July 2026 from your recurring items" button on the dashboard hero itself rather than a message pointing at another page.

#### [CRITICAL] Amount fields require the user to literally type the "RM" prefix and exactly two decimals, on a numeric keypad that cannot produce letters
`src/features/expenses/expense-view.tsx:36`

**Problem.** Every money input in the app is a text input with `pattern="RM(?:0|[1-9][0-9]*)\.[0-9]{2}"` `placeholder="RM0.00"` `inputMode="decimal"` — expense-view.tsx:36-43, shared-bill-view.tsx:26-33, monthly-plan-view.tsx:70-77 and 295-303. `inputMode="decimal"` opens a digits-and-separator keypad on mobile, which physically cannot type `R` or `M`, so on the primary target platform the field can only be satisfied by editing the placeholder-shaped default or by switching keyboards. The pattern also rejects `RM12.5`, `RM 12.50`, `12.50`, `RM012.50` and `RM1,250.00`. The strictness is inherited from `parseRM` (src/domain/money.ts:4) being used directly as the form contract instead of a lenient parser at the boundary.

**Impact.** This is the "functions a bit not user friendly to key in" complaint in its purest form. Recording an expense — the action the product needs to happen in seconds, several times a day — starts with a fight over currency syntax. On mobile it is close to unusable without keyboard switching.

**Fix.** Keep integer-sen storage and keep `parseRM` as the domain contract, but stop making the user produce its input format. Render the field as `inputMode="decimal"` with a non-editable `RM` prefix rendered as adjacent text (not inside the value), accept `12`, `12.5`, `12.50`, `1,250` and normalise to `RM1250.00` in a small `coerceRMInput()` helper before calling `parseRM`. Format on blur so the user sees the canonical value. Drop the HTML `pattern` in favour of the coercion plus a real inline error.

#### [CRITICAL] Any invalid input replaces the whole page with the generic error boundary; no field ever shows an inline error
`src/features/plan/actions.ts:142`

**Problem.** Server actions validate by throwing: `throw new Error('Invalid monthly plan template')` (src/features/plan/actions.ts:142, :148), `throw new Error('Invalid personal expense')` (src/features/expenses/actions.ts:52, :89), plus several bare `throw new Error()` (plan/actions.ts:115, :126, :210, :215). Nothing in the app uses `useActionState`/`useFormState` — a repo-wide grep finds zero occurrences, and the only `role="alert"` outside the sign-in page is inside ResolutionEditor's client-side allocation preview (resolution-editor.tsx:347). So a rejected amount or a missing effective-start date unmounts the page and renders app/(app)/error.tsx: "We could not load your financial plan / Your financial data was not displayed. Please try again."

**Impact.** A typo in one field produces a scary data-loss-sounding error page, loses every other field the user typed, and never says which field was wrong or why. Combined with the RM-prefix requirement above, the most common expense-entry path ends in this screen.

**Fix.** Convert each server action to return a typed result (`{ ok: false, fieldErrors: { amount: 'Enter an amount like 12.50' } }`) instead of throwing on user-input validation, consume it with `useActionState`, and render the message next to the offending field with `aria-describedby` + `aria-invalid`. Reserve throwing (and the error boundary) for genuine infrastructure failures. Note the interaction with DraftForm: it currently calls `clearDraft` unconditionally after `await action(formData)` (src/features/forms/draft-form.tsx:57-62), so a validation result must not clear the draft.

### Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA)

_28 findings._ Judged against WCAG 2.2 AA this codebase is well-intentioned but fails at the level of systems rather than details: the HTML semantics are unusually good in places (every form control has a real `<label>` — implicit wrapping throughout the feature views, explicit `htmlFor`/`id` on sign-in — there is no placeholder-as-label anti-pattern anywhere, `aria-labelledby` correctly ties sections to their headings, `scope` is on the one table, and `<time dateTime>` is used consistently), yet three whole categories of accessibility are simply absent. There is no focus indicator anywhere (zero `:focus`/`:focus-visible` rules in globals.css), no error identification anywhere (zero `aria-live`, `aria-invalid`, `aria-describedby` or `aria-errormessage` in the entire tree — validation failures throw from server actions and are swallowed by the error boundary at app/(app)/error.tsx, destroying the user's typed data), and no status messaging anywhere (nothing announces a successful save, delete or resolution, and focus is lost after every list mutation). On colour I want to be precise and give credit where due: the **text** tokens actually pass comfortably — `--muted #8ea6b8` measures 7.2:1 on `--panel #0b1621`, 7.5:1 on the translucent `rgba(4,11,18,0.36)` card and 7.3:1 on the metric/warning panel (its weakest position is 4.8:1 against the lightest end of the hero gradient, still passing); `#c8d9e6` nav links are 13.3:1; `--blue #55c7ff` as text is 10.0:1 on the page and 6.4:1 on the hero; `#ffe1a3` on the warning panel is 14.5:1; body `--text` is 17.7:1. Nothing fails 1.4.3. What fails is **1.4.11 non-text contrast**, and it fails badly: `--line rgba(84,187,255,0.22)` on every input, select and textarea measures 1.6:1 against the field interior and 1.5:1 against the page (3:1 required), and the table cell borders at `rgba(84,187,255,0.16)` are 1.34:1 — so the form fields are effectively invisible, which is almost certainly the concrete cause of the user's "UI not correct" complaint. The other structural failures are `display: block` on `<table>` (globals.css:255), which silently strips the table role and renders the correct `scope` attributes inert; an identical `<title>` on all ten routes (a Level A 2.4.2 failure); no skip link past 9-15 repeated nav links; and target sizes under the 24px 2.5.8 minimum for the two controls the app leans on hardest — `<summary>` at ~18px (the *only* way to reach every edit and delete form) and checkbox labels at ~19px (20+ of them in the bill resolver). Finally, on the PWA: the manifest's cream `#f7f4ed` background and green `#173f35` theme colour bear no relationship to the near-black `#061018` UI, and there is no `themeColor` viewport export at all — so the installed app flashes a white splash into a black interface on every cold start.

#### [CRITICAL] No focus indicator is defined anywhere in the app
`app/globals.css:54`

**Problem.** globals.css contains zero `:focus`, `:focus-visible`, or `:focus-within` rules — grep across app/ and src/ returns no match for "focus" at all.
